Near the coast and you feel a strong quake, or the sea pulls back?

  1. Go now. Don't wait for a siren, an app or the news. Nature's warning is often the only one.
  2. Go up and inland: at least 30 m (100 ft) high or 3 km (2 miles) inland. On foot if roads are jammed.
  3. No high ground near? Go to the 4th floor or higher of a strong concrete building (last resort).
  4. Stay there for hours. Waves come in a series and the first is often not the biggest. Wait for an official all-clear.
  5. Never go down to look at the sea or the retreating water.

Natural warning signs

"Long or strong, get gone."

  • Shaking that makes it hard to stand, or that lasts 20 seconds or more.
  • The sea suddenly drains away and exposes the seabed, reefs or fish (this happened in 2004).
  • A loud roar from the ocean, like a train or jet.
  • A fast rise of water or an unusual "wall" of water on the horizon.

A distant earthquake can send a tsunami with no shaking at all where you are. That's why official alerts and this app's push notifications matter too.

When you arrive at a beach hotel

  • Ask the front desk: "Where is the tsunami evacuation route and assembly point?"
  • Look for blue/white tsunami evacuation signs and walk the route once.
  • Find the nearest high ground or tall concrete building, and how long it takes to walk there.
  • Agree on a family meeting point on high ground, in case you are separated (e.g. one at the beach, one in the room).
  • Keep phones charged; carry ID, a copy of passports and a little cash in a small bag.
  • Save the local emergency numbers below and the hotel's phone number.

Before the trip

  • Turn on Emergency / Government alerts on every phone (iPhone: Settings → Notifications → bottom; Android: Settings → Safety & emergency → Wireless emergency alerts).
  • Install ntfy and subscribe to the family topic (see the README) so you get this app's alerts.
  • Install the official app of the country you're visiting (table below).
  • Share your itinerary and hotel names with someone at home.
  • Register your trip with your embassy if your country offers it.

During and after a quake

  • Drop, cover, hold on until the shaking stops. Then, if near the coast, evacuate immediately.
  • Expect aftershocks. Stay away from damaged buildings, bridges and power lines.
  • Use text messages instead of calls; networks overload.
  • If caught in water: grab something that floats. Don't try to swim against the current.

What this app can and can't do

  • Nobody can predict earthquakes. This app detects them after they happen and relays official tsunami bulletins.
  • Alerts can arrive 5–30 minutes late (free scheduled checks). A tsunami near the source can arrive faster than that.
  • So: your own eyes and the natural signs come first, then local sirens and authorities, then this app.
